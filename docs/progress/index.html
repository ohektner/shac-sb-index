The model today

What the model is right now

Every prediction starts from Bart Torvik's team ratings. Each team has an offensive and a defensive efficiency: points scored and allowed per 100 possessions against an average D-I team. The model turns two teams' ratings into a predicted margin.

predicted home margin = slope × (rating gap × possessions ÷ 100) + home court
ExampleHome team net rating (offense − defense)+18.0 Away team net rating+6.0 Rating gap, points per 100 possessions12.0 Expected possessions (both teams' tempo)68 Rating part: 12.0 × 68 ÷ 1008.2 × slope 1.01, + home court 3.4+3.5 Predicted: home team by11.7

Is it different from existing models? Not yet.

The Torvik baseline is that same formula with the slope fixed at 1 and home court fit to past seasons. Our model learns both numbers. On real data the slope came out at 1.00 to 1.03 and home court at 3.3 to 3.5 points, which is almost exactly the baseline.

We also tried four extra inputs: early-season ratings weighted differently, rest days, offense and defense weighted separately, and a separate home court for conference games. None helped enough to pass the rule set before the test. Torvik's ratings already contain what those inputs know.

That is a real finding, and an expected one. Re-weighting public ratings won't beat a market that already uses them. An edge has to come from information the ratings don't have.

The differentiators

What will make it different

G2

An AI that reads availability news

Ratings don't know that a star sprained an ankle this morning. An AI model reads injury reports and news, flags rotation players who are out or questionable, and adjusts the margin for new absences only. Most public models don't do this, and none measure the AI's accuracy in public.

Bars, fixed in advance: 95% reading accuracy, and 95% precision on "out," checked against box scores.

G1

An honest, checkable record

Metrics and Pick/Lean cutoffs were written down before any result existed. Every call is committed to a public ledger before tip, and the ledger can't be edited. Losses stay on the record.

Most picks sites can't show that. For a portfolio, it's what makes the results believable.

G3

A market study

We save Pinnacle (the sharpest book), 8 US books and Polymarket several times a day. That supports two questions: does our number predict which way the line moves, and how do sharp books, US books and a prediction market differ?

The statistics

What we measure, and why each one

Every metric is reported for our model and for the Torvik baseline, on the same games, season by season and pooled over the four test seasons (2022-23 to 2025-26).

MeasureWhat it meansWhy we use it
PrimaryDistance to the closeAverage gap, in points, between our predicted margin and the closing line.The closing line is the best public forecast there is. Getting closer to it than Torvik does is the pass/fail test for G1. It's much less noisy than final scores.
PrimaryCLV (closing line value)Did the line move toward our side between when we priced it and tip? Measured in points.The standard test of whether a bettor is sharp. A model that keeps beating the close is likely right, even through a losing streak.
ContextError vs. final scoresAverage miss against the actual margin, shown next to the closing line's own miss.Easy to understand, but one game can miss by 20 for no reason, so small differences mean little.
ContextATS record at −110Win % picking against the spread. Pushes are listed separately.Break-even at −110 is 110 ÷ 210 = 52.4%. Reported, but needs thousands of games to mean anything.
CalibrationBrier score and reliability tableWhen the model says 56%, does that side win about 56% of the time? Grouped in 2-point bands.Tiers are only honest if the probabilities behind them are.
TiersPick and Lean recordCalls, win %, a 95% Wilson interval, gap to 52.4% and mean CLV for each tier.The interval shows when a hot start is really just a small sample. If no tier beats 52.4%, the scorecard says so.
MethodWhat it doesWhy this one
Least squaresFinds the slope and home-court numbers that make predicted margins closest to real ones.The simplest fit there is, and easy to explain. Fancier methods (ridge, boosted trees) get tried only if they beat it.
Walk-forward by seasonTo predict 2024-25, the model is fit only on earlier seasons.That is exactly what live use looks like. Mixing seasons would leak the future.
Logistic calibrationMaps "edge in points" to "chance this side covers," learned from past seasons.The textbook normal-curve formula overstates confidence. Logistic is stable on small samples.
Tier cutoffsPick at 56%+ calibrated cover chance; Lean at 53% to 56%; otherwise no call.Well above the 52.4% break-even, so a call has room to be wrong a little and still be worth making.
2020-21 set asideNever trained on and kept out of the headline numbers; reported on its own.No fans meant home court nearly vanished that season.
The process

How the calculations get decided

The rule underneath everything: decide how you'll be judged before you see how you did. The steps below happen in this order.

  1. Start from standard practice.CLV and distance to the Pinnacle or US-book close are the industry's own yardsticks. Torvik's formula is the published standard for turning ratings into a margin.
  2. Write it down first.Metrics, test seasons and tier cutoffs went into the pre-registration file on Nov 9, before any data was pulled. Git history proves the date.
  3. Start with the simplest model.The baseline formula with learned weights. Anything added has to earn its place.
  4. Choose inputs on training seasons only.Candidate inputs are compared on 2018-19, 2019-20 and 2021-22. The rule for keeping one was written before the comparison ran: at least 2 standard errors better, and better in every season. The four test seasons have no say.
  5. Test once on held-out seasons.After task 1.2 fixes the closing line, the scorecard runs once on 2022-23 to 2025-26. Those are the numbers that get reported.
  6. Log every choice.Each choice goes in DECISIONS.md with the options considered and why. Changing a rule later needs a dated entry, applied only going forward.
